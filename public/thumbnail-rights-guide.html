Using Video Thumbnails Responsibly

Practical considerations before reusing a public video thumbnail in your own work.

A thumbnail may be visible to anyone on a public video page, but visibility is not the same as permission to reuse it. The image may be protected by copyright, trademark or other rights, and it may include artwork, photographs, logos or people.

Personal reference versus republication

Saving an image for private reference, research or organizing your own project is different from publishing it on a website, using it in an advertisement or including it in a monetized video. The rules can depend on your location, the purpose, the amount used and the rights involved.

Ask the rights holder

If you plan to republish a thumbnail, contact the creator or rights holder and request permission. Keep a record of the permission and any conditions, such as attribution, platform or duration. If you cannot identify the rights holder, consider using an image you created or properly licensed instead.

Do not imply endorsement

Using a creator's thumbnail or channel branding in a way that suggests they sponsor or endorse your project can mislead viewers. Make the source clear and avoid altering images in a deceptive way.

SnapYT's role

SnapYT provides access to thumbnail files that are publicly available for a video. It does not grant a licence or transfer ownership. Users are responsible for checking permissions and following applicable laws and platform terms.
